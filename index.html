E-100  ·  Background

What I actually do

My day job is construction documents: power distribution, lighting and fire alarm design against the NEC and NFPA 72, for buildings that get built. It is unglamorous and it is the best possible training in the thing engineering actually rewards — being correct, on a deadline, where being wrong is expensive.

The rest of my time goes to hardware that moves. Firmware on ESP32 and Arduino, Verilog on MAX 10, control systems, drone autonomy, and the software around all of it. My graduate capstone was a drone swarm that maps wildfire fronts for the crews fighting them — an interdisciplinary team, a real problem, and the clearest answer I have found to what I want to build.

The thread back to the start is a talking trash can. PUSH, at Walt Disney World, convinced a kid that a machine could have a personality and that somebody had to build it. That is still the target: robotics that people react to, not just machines that function.

I take work where at least two of three hold — good people, meaningful work, fair compensation. It has been a reliable filter so far.
