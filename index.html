E-100  ·  Background

What I actually do

My day job is systems engineering at building scale. At IMEG I perform system-level electrical analysis, redundancy planning and safety-critical design to NEC and NFPA standards, then produce the wiring diagrams, load paths and electrical architecture documentation that a building actually gets built from — coordinated across every other discipline on the project. It is the best training there is in what engineering rewards: being correct, on a deadline, where being wrong is expensive.

The rest of my time goes to autonomous systems — integrating hardware, software and sensors from concept through testing. My graduate capstone was a drone swarm that maps wildfire fronts for the crews fighting them, and the swarm algorithm behind it was published at IEEE SII 2026.

The thread back to the start is a talking trash can. PUSH, at Walt Disney World, convinced a kid that a machine could have a personality and that somebody had to build it. That is still the target. I want to make people feel like a kid again — to feel that wonder, and to believe, for a moment, in the impossible. In magic.
